A linear mixed-effects model was fitted to ERP amplitudes in the N250 time window, with fixed effects of sensitivity to orthographic structure, morphological family size, morphological complexity, and their interactions. Laterality and anteriority were included as nuisance covariates to control for scalp distribution effects, and random intercepts were estimated for participants. Degrees of freedom and p-values were computed using the Kenward-Roger approximation. The model explained 42.1\% of the total variance ($R^2_\text{conditional} = .421$), with fixed effects accounting for 2.5\% of the variance ($R^2_\text{marginal} = .025$).

No significant main effects were found for sensitivity to orthographic structure, family size, or complexity ($p\text{s} > .48$), nor for the nuisance variables laterality and anteriority. However, two significant two-way interactions emerged.

First, the interaction between sensitivity to orthographic structure and morphological family size was significant, $F(1, 2121) = 4.52$, $p = .034$, $\eta^2_{\text{partial}} = .0021$. Although no individual pairwise comparisons were statistically significant after Bonferroni correction, the estimated marginal means revealed a crossover pattern. In the High sensitivity condition, ERP amplitudes were numerically more positive for large family size items (–0.37 μV, 95\% CI [–1.09, 0.35]) than for small family size items (–0.64 μV, 95\% CI [–1.36, 0.08]). In contrast, in the Low sensitivity condition, ERP amplitudes were more negative for large family size items (–0.74 μV, 95\% CI [–1.41, –0.08]) than for small family size items (–0.61 μV, 95\% CI [–1.27, 0.06]).

Second, the interaction between sensitivity to orthographic structure and morphological complexity was also significant, $F(1, 2121) = 6.14$, $p = .013$, $\eta^2_{\text{partial}} = .0029$. While no individual contrasts were significant after correction ($p\text{s} > .08$), the pattern of estimated marginal means again showed a crossover. In the High sensitivity condition, complex items elicited more negative amplitudes (–0.61 μV, 95\% CI [–1.33, 0.11]) than simple items (–0.40 μV, 95\% CI [–1.12, 0.32]). In contrast, in the Low sensitivity condition, complex items were slightly more positive (–0.54 μV, 95\% CI [–1.21, 0.12]) than simple items (–0.81 μV, 95\% CI [–1.47, –0.14]).

The three-way interaction among sensitivity to orthographic structure, morphological family size, and complexity was not significant, $F(1, 2121) = 0.14$, $p = .708$, $\eta^2_{\text{partial}} < .0001$.

Although partial eta-squared values were small, this is typical in ERP studies where a substantial proportion of amplitude variance is attributable to topographic variables such as laterality and anteriority. These variables were included to account for scalp distribution effects and to improve the specificity of estimates for the linguistic predictors of interest.

## 🔍 Study Focus

This study investigates how two key morphological variables — **base frequency** and **morphological family size** — influence **reaction times (RTs)** in a **lexical decision task (LDT)**. All items (including nonwords) were morphologically structured, and the focus was on whether these two variables show **additive effects**, suggesting **distinct cognitive processing mechanisms**.

---

## 📊 Main Findings

- Both **base frequency** and **morphological family size** had **significant, additive effects** on RTs.
- Each accounted for a similar proportion of variance (η² ≈ 0.1).
- Additive pattern suggests **independent processing stages or mechanisms**.

---

## 🧠 Theoretical Interpretations

### A. Additive Factors Method (AFM) Interpretation

- Additive effects are consistent with the **Sternberg (1969)** logic of **independent serial stages**.
- Hypothesized mapping:
  - **Base frequency** → early **lexical access** (form-based).
  - **Family size** → later **semantic co-activation** or post-lexical resonance.

### B. Diffusion Decision Model (DDM) Perspective

- Although not explicitly modeled, the effects are **theoretically mappable** to DDM parameters:
  - **Base frequency** may affect **drift rate (v)**.
  - **Family size** may affect **non-decision time (Ter)** or boundary separation (a).
- Current analysis used **LME**, not DDM, which remains standard and valid. Acknowledging DDM concepts provides **process-level depth** without requiring a full reanalysis.
